What the signal covers

  • Exposure yes/no - does this email appear in known public breaches.
  • Which breaches - name, month, and what data classes were involved (password hash, address, phone).
  • Recent? - leaks from the last 90 days matter most; that's the stuffing window.
  • Freshness as contrast - zero-history inboxes used heavily are a different species from bent old accounts; yute returns both shapes with evidence attached.

Why does breach history matter for fraud?

Credential stuffing works because people reuse passwords. An email with recent leak exposure is an account-takeover and fake-signup magnet; a brand-new inbox with zero history behaves very differently. The signal shifts the risk model - it doesn't decide it alone.

Is checking an email privacy-compliant?

yute breach surfaces only public data-breach datasets - the same sources security researchers reference - and returns facts about the dataset, not anyone's password. For password checks specifically, industry-standard k-anonymity hashing means raw credentials are never shared or stored.

Is this just Have I Been Pwned?

Purely public-password-leak data is one input. yute breach is meant to mix that with the rest of the identity signals (email legitimacy, domain age, web presence) in the same lookup and the same credit - one call, one story.
